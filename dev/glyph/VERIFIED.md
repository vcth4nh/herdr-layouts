# glyph: what has been verified

What each part of glyph was checked with, and what was never tried. The tools named here are described in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), and their paths are relative to `dev/glyph/`.

- **Seen in real frames:**
  - The rail, labelled and with a real fleet, including the row of empty machines.
  - The bar and titled strip with 2 and 7 tabs.
  - The pane header and all four states.
  - A pretended drag filmed in the real strip (stand-in bar.js, 3 s): the lifted tab stayed on top, the others slid under it and aside, and it returned cleanly at the end.
  - The chevrons on the machines' lines, the shell logo on the plain-terminal launcher, a tab red after its first close press, and a project's empty page.
  - The Go-to list over a real fleet, opened by its command: on the shown tab and scrolled to it, with agents' titles where Herdr's picker had numbers. Again on 2026-11-19 (herdr-app 0.1.16): each project's tabs standing in under its name beside the line in its hue, the line of the project on show the brightest, and the picked row raised beside it.
  - The empty page of a project whose only shell had exited (2026-11-19, a frame `tools/close-pass.py` took): the project still on show under its own name, with no tab in the rail or the bar.
  - Sub-agent rows under a real tab: two helpers started with `hc run`, each on its hook, one at work (the arc, a bright name) and one resting (dim).
  - The transfer window over a real fleet (`tools/transfer-pass.sh`, 2026-11-14): both sides read, a row picked with its dashed landing row and the arrow, a 150 MB copy under way (the pill and its percent, the arc around the bar's button, the file growing on the other side), then the notice and the whole file in the other folder.
  - The direct way between two real servers (`transfer-pass.sh direct`, 2026-11-14): the bolt once both had answered, a picked row with the machine's name and the field reading "password", dots in the field for a made-up word with the accent on its border and the bolt, the peach pill of a copy standing at its prompt, and the pill at work after the window typed a made-up word into a pretend prompt.
  - Picking in the transfer window (`transfer-pass.sh pick`, 2026-11-15), played through the window's own handlers: three swept rows and their number on the arrow, a fourth row added, the picked rows faint with the chip by the pretended pointer and the other list filled, the arrow and the row it would replace red after the rows were let go on a name that was there, and three files in the other folder after the second carry.
  - The empty part of a list (a stand-in that wrote its measured size in the window's head, 2026-11-15): 3px tall under 14 rows, and 0 under a list that scrolls.
  - The bars and the time after the names of four real machines (2026-11-16): three bars each, and times in the range `hc exec --on <machine> true` took on them when timed in a shell. The rail has run `whoami` since 2026-11-20, which took as long as `true` on five real machines when both were timed in a shell that day. With that rail installed, two frames of that day had bars and a time after the names of four real machines: 39ms for the nearest, and 270ms to 320ms for the others.
  - The chevron beside every project's tile over a real fleet (2026-11-19, herdr-app 0.1.16), under the machines' chevrons and clear of the shown project's bar.
  - Folded projects (a stand-in rail.js that started with four of nine folded, 3 s, 2026-11-19): each with its tile, its name and the chevron pointing right, the shown project among them with its bar. Under each name was the strip of states that the status line has since replaced. In the frame taken after the real file was back, every project was open again.
  - A project that had been folded by hand in the real window (2026-11-19): still folded in the frame taken after the mod was loaded again.
  - The status lines over a real fleet, with made-up states (`tools/line-pass.sh`, three films of six seconds, 2026-11-19, herdr-app 0.1.16):
    - One bead for each tab that has a state, by state, with the ones that wanted you 8px wide and the mark under the bead of the tab on show. Within a state the beads were then in order of how long each tab had waited, which the rail's own order has since replaced.
    - A bead that turned peach slid to the ones that need you in about 0.4 s, over the beads it passed, while the others closed up and the mark followed its own bead. One that came to rest slid under the others.
    - The same slide took as long while the rail was drawn again every 45 ms. The turning arc of a working logo and the pulse of a blocked one went on through those redraws. A glint starts again from its bead's near end when the bead's width changes.
    - A bead under the pretended pointer grew taller, and its tab's row was lit. A second bead grew as the first went back.
    - A folded machine's line had its beads in the room after its name and bars, and a folded project's under its name.
    - The second film was cut short after three seconds: another checkout's install replaced the stand-in. The script left those files alone and said so.
    - Since 2026-11-20 the script films nothing when another installed file of the mod is not this checkout's. It was seen to refuse once, with no installed file touched. No film was made after that was added.
  - The status line with the fleet's real states, once the files were installed (2026-11-19): the beads by state, the glint 1px inside its bead with the bead's ends round, and no error in `herdr-app status`.
    - One frame had a row under a real pointer: the row lit and its bead taller.
    - One frame caught a real change of state as it began: a bead already green, still at the place and the width it had while working.
    - All of the above was the mod as it was before it brought its own fonts and timed machines with `whoami`. With both installed (2026-11-20), a frame had the line on top and a folded project's line with real states, and `herdr-app status` had no error.
  - The beads of one state in the rail's order (2026-11-20, a frame of the real fleet after that order was installed): three tabs were working, the one on show was the third of them down the rail, and the mark was under the third blue bead. By how long each had worked it could not have been third: another of the three had started after it. The other states' beads were not told apart in a real frame.
  - The rail at rest with a real fleet, after the drag of projects, machines and tabs was installed (2026-11-20): drawn as before, with no error in `herdr-app status`. No drag has been seen in a real frame.
- **Checked in the real app by commands** (`tools/close-pass.py`: 30 passed, 2026-11-19, herdr-app 0.1.16, on throwaway projects of this computer):
  - One press closes a shell and two close a running program. A second press after 3 s counts as a first.
  - The tab after the closed one is shown, or the one before it for the last tab.
  - While the project on show has one tab, a page stands by behind it, put away (`settled` in `herdr-app ls`), and the tab stays the one shown. A project that is left keeps its tab and no page.
  - A last tab leaves its project on its empty page, with the name and folder kept. A new tab from the page starts in that folder, and the page is put away again behind it. Leaving the page, or the close key on it, closes the project.
  - A lone shell told to `exit` leaves its project on show on that page, with the name and folder kept. A new tab from the page starts in the folder the shell was in.
  - When the shown project closes, from Herdr's own close or the close key on its page, the next project on the same computer shows its first tab.
  - The close key on the page of this computer's last project shows the last tab of the project before it. Projects of other machines came next in Herdr's list, and none came on show.
- **Checked against real machines by commands** (`tools/copy-pass.sh`: 21 passed, 2026-11-14, with one macOS and one Linux machine):
  - Each machine's own `sh` and `ls` list a folder the way `copy.js` reads it, sizes included.
  - Files and a folder with spaces, brackets, braces, `*`, `?`, `$`, quotes, a backslash, a colon and accents in their names copy from this computer to a machine, from that machine to another through here, and back, and come back byte for byte.
  - A copy that ends well is told and its terminal removed. One whose source is missing is told as failed with scp's last line, stays listed, and goes when dismissed.
- **Checked against real machines by commands, for the direct way** (`tools/direct-pass.sh`: 10 passed for each of three pairs, 2026-11-14):
  - scp started on a Linux server, on a Linux machine that fetches because the other can't reach it, and on a Mac each stood at the other machine's password prompt.
  - Ended there, the copy is told as failed, nothing has been copied, and its terminal is removed.
  - With another machine's host key in place of the right one, ssh refuses before any prompt.
  - A word typed with `hc send` into a pretend prompt on the machine that ran scp arrives, and the screen does not show it.
  - `--survey` answered for every two of five machines.
- **Checked in the real app by commands sent by hand** (`herdr-app do glyph.goto`, then `herdr-app status`, 2026-11-14): the command opens the Go-to list, and sent again it closes it. After the list was closed from outside (`herdr-app do overlay.close`), the next one opened it.
  - The same way on 2026-11-15: `glyph.transfer` sent four times opened the transfer window, closed it, opened it and closed it. `glyph.transfer-show` opened it, and sent again left the one window open.
  - The same way on 2026-11-20: `space.move` and `tab.move` with the index the rail gives, on throwaway projects ("Tabs and projects" in the platform doc). The rail's own drag was not.
  - The same way on 2026-11-19, with herdr-app 0.1.16: with Herdr's peek open on a throwaway `sleep` that had no pane (`herdr-app do terminal.peek`), `glyph.close` and then `glyph.close-pane` each closed the peek. `hc get` still called the `sleep` live, and in the frame taken half a second later the tab under it was not red.
  - Herdr's part in the page that stands by for a project's one tab, the same day, on a throwaway project that was never on show, with one of Herdr's own mods as the page: a tab made with `focus: false` and put away with `tab.settle` left the window on the project, tab and pane it was on. Once the project's shell had exited, that tab was the one its project had left, and the one it showed ("Tabs and projects" in the platform doc). glyph's bar had no part in it: the installed one was the bar from before this change.
- **Read back from the real Dock** (`lsappinfo info -only StatusLabel -app Herdr`, 2026-11-19, herdr-app 0.1.16): Herdr's icon had no label before the bar with the badge was installed, and the label 2 within a second after, the number on the bar's bell in a frame taken then. The label was still 2 after files of the mod were saved three more times, each of which loads the mod again.
- **Checked offline by the logic tests** (`logic/run.sh`, all passing on 2026-11-20), which call the mod's handlers with made-up events against a fake app. Outside the transfer window this is the only testing clicks, drags and menus have had.
  - `bar-test.mjs`, 60 passed: a click on a tab, a tab dragged to another place, off the strip and back, a release that is never heard, renaming by double-click and by F2, and the right-click menu.
  - `rail-test.mjs`, 216 passed: the rail, its status lines, sub-agent rows, the folds, moving projects, machines and tabs, the tab menu, the pane header, folders on a Windows machine, and the face for bold letters on each system.
    - Moving: a press that stays a click, the 5px before a drag, where the line goes over a project, over a machine's line, over a folded machine and over its last project, nowhere over another machine's projects or the shelf, the `space.move` a project's drop asks for, the line kept until the move is there or 2 s pass, the order of machines a machine's drop writes and every list following it, `agent.next`'s order, a drop 400 ms after the pointer left the rail, a hold that hears nothing for 15 s, the tile, name, folded block and bead clicks that follow a drag not counting, a project alone on its machine, the logos-only rail, and a machine the order does not name.
    - Moving tabs: the `tab.move` a drop asks for, the line over or under a tab's row, under the last of its sub-agents' rows, and beside a logo in the logos-only rail, a sub-agent's row counting as its tab, first over the project's name, nowhere over another project, the tab and its sub-agents faint, the row's and the logo's click after a drag not counting, and a project's only tab.
    - That face: Avenir Next on a Mac and until Herdr says which system it is, Nunito Sans on Linux and Windows from the mod's first draw, and the two files in the mod's `fonts/` folder being that family, a 600 and a 700, with their license beside them.
    - The status lines: the order of the beads, by state and then as the tabs' rows stand, whatever their times; the bar's bell and tick stepping through a state's beads from left to right; how a line's width is shared, in a crowd and with beads that keep 8px; each state's color and motion, and the pulse that stands in for a glint on a narrow line; the mark of the tab on show and its 14px limit; the order the beads are drawn in.
    - A click and a right-click on a bead, which the block around a folded project's line does not hear. The pointer on a bead and on a row or a logo: which bead grows on which lines, which row is lit, how often the rail is drawn again, and a tab that goes while it is pointed at.
    - What slides and when: nothing the first time a line is drawn or after its width changed, a bead and the mark from then on, and a bead whose tab changed state taking its new place under its old name. The measured width, at zoom 1 and 1.25, and the rail's own width after a resize.
    - A project's fold: the rows that go and the status line that takes their place, in the labelled rail and the logos-only one, the chevron and the tile folding and their hover words, what shows the project (its name, folded or not, and the block around a folded one's line), the right-click menu on the tile and on that block, a folded machine around it, a project without a state or without a tab, the fold kept for the next rail, and forgotten once the project is gone and every machine is in reach.
    - A machine's bars: which machines are asked and with what, an answer that fails, the three answers kept and what they come to, the bars, the time and the hover words on a line and on a pill, how wide the machine's name may be beside them, and the rail drawn again only when what it shows changes.
    - Folders on a Windows machine: home as `~`, a path cut at its own slash and any other at `/` only, the label of a tab that goes by its folder, and the pane header's folder with the whole way on hover.
  - `close-test.mjs`, 144 passed: the bar's closing, the empty page, the tab that shows when the project on show closes, the start keys, a close key with a peek on show, and the Dock badge.
    - The Dock badge: the number at the bar's first look, told again only when it changes, a tab that is done not counted, the number said once more two seconds after the start, no badge when no tab is blocked, the badge taken off as the bar goes, and nothing set under `herdr-app check`.
    - The project on show closing, by the close key on its page and from Herdr's side: a machine's first, middle, last and only project, the list's last one, a project left with only its page in the way, and a machine out of reach.
    - The page that stands by: asked for once for the one tab of the project on show, at the second look that finds it on show, not shown, and put away once it is there. None for a project with more tabs or one that is not on show. It goes when the project is left or gets a second tab, and is asked for again when the project is down to one tab on show again.
    - A last tab that ends by itself leaves the project on show as that page, by the name and folder noted while the tab was there. The close key on the one tab shows the page and closes the tab at once, and waits for a page that is still on its way.
    - What the page will show is written only when it changes. The page on show beside the tab hands back to the tab. A page that never comes is asked for again, and one that something else closed only a minute later. A look that goes wrong is said once and does not end the looking.
    - Both rules at once: the project Herdr shows for a moment after another closed gets no page, and the one the bar moves the window to gets its page a look later.
  - `goto-test.mjs`, 54 passed: what the Go-to list lists and by which words, typing, the arrows, Enter, Ctrl+D, its height, and the bar opening and closing it. Also what says whose tab a row is: how far the rows stand in, each project's line (its place, its hue, brighter for the project on show, where it ends, with a word typed too), and the room over a project's and a machine's line.
  - `keys-test.mjs`, 113 passed: that `keys.json` gives every command one key on macOS and one on Linux and Windows, none of them a key Herdr's terminal keeps there, a plain Ctrl+letter or Ctrl+Alt, and that a key Herdr binds is only taken for the glyph command doing the same job. Also the keys the hover words show on each system, and Herdr's own keys standing in for missing ones.
  - `transfer-test.mjs`, 199 passed: the transfer window, the scp command a copy becomes, and the bar's part in a copy. It also lists a folder it makes with this computer's own `sh` and `ls`, the way copy.js asks a machine to.
    - Picking: a click, ⇧ and ⌘ clicks, a sweep, a carry and where it is let go, a hold whose release is never heard, and the bar closing the window on show.
    - The direct way: what copy.js asks ssh and reads back, which machine runs scp, that scp's arguments, and the password, which is typed once per prompt, never after a refusal, emptied from the field, and gone when the window closes.
    - The password field: on show before any row is picked, whose password it is for, and the press that gives it the keyboard.
    - What says which way a copy goes: the arrow's badge, the pill and the notice.
    - scp's meter: the speed, the time left and a stalled copy.
    - The window asking for the keyboard as it opens, Esc, and the answer about two machines being kept for the next window.

With a page standing by behind a project's one tab, the bar's tree has that one tab and the rail's that one row.

- **Tried by hand with a real pointer and keys** (2026-11-15), in the transfer window: picking rows (a click, ⇧ and ⌘ clicks, a sweep), carrying rows to the other side and letting go, typing in the password field after a click on it, ⇧⌘T closing the window while it holds the keyboard, and Esc.
- **Never tried with a real pointer or key press:** hover tooltips, clicks (a sub-agent row's peek among them), right-click menus, a real drag of a tab, scrolling the strip, F2 and the other bound keys (the live pass sends their commands, not the keys), ⌘W with a peek on show, a peek opened by a click, folding a machine, a click on a folded project's name, a click or a right-click on a bead of a status line, dragging a project, a machine or a tab in the rail, and the launchers on the empty page. The pointer on a row was seen once to make its bead taller (above). The pointer on a bead, and a bead's hover words, were not. In the transfer window: a press on a list's empty part, a drop from Finder, a pill's peek, and dragging the window. Of a machine's bars: their hover words. Typing in the Go-to list, its arrows, Enter, Ctrl+D and ⌘P itself were not tried either. If something is reported broken, start there.
- **Never tried in the real app, of the status lines:** Herdr's zoom other than 1, where a measured width is divided by the zoom. The rail resized by its edge, or switched to 160px, with a line on show. A crowd whose beads stand 1px apart or touch, and the pulse that stands in for a glint there. A slide of the mark under a bead wider than 14px.
- **Never tried in the real app, of the page that stands by:** whether scrolling the bar and Herdr's own tab keys skip it, which pane has the keyboard while it is made (the check reads the shown tab only), a tab that ends before its page is there, a page closed by Herdr on another computer and the minute the bar then waits, and a project on another machine. A throw inside the bar's look was not provoked in the app either: what Herdr does with one is not known.
- **Never tried in the real app, of a project closing:** the last project of another machine, where `tools/close-pass.py` makes none, and a machine's last project closing from Herdr's own menu, which is in no tool. There the window is on another machine's project for a moment before the bar moves it back, and how that moment looks was not seen.
- **Never tried in the real app, of the Dock badge:** the number changing as a tab becomes blocked or is answered, the badge coming off when no tab is blocked, and another layout shown, which should take it off. How the badge looks on the icon was not seen either: only its label was read.
- **Never run on Linux or Windows:** glyph as a whole. The fonts glyph brings were never drawn by Herdr on any system: the offline pictures draw them in a browser. What a transfer window's side says on a Windows computer without `sh` is from `transfer-test.mjs` only. Folders on a Windows machine were checked by `rail-test.mjs` only, on made-up paths: what Herdr reports as a folder there was not seen. That the Dock badge's call does nothing there is from herdr-app's source, not from a run. Its keys there were picked from herdr-app's source and checked only by `keys-test.mjs`. No key has been pressed there, and how a desktop's own Ctrl+Shift keys meet them was not looked at.
- **Never run to its end:** a direct copy with a real password. Every step up to the prompt, and the typing into a pretend one, was tried. A real login, the files landing and a wrong password were not. `direct-pass.sh A B --ask` runs the copy for whoever has the password.
